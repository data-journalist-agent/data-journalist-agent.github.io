14The map under the music

15Across every grand-final country-to-country vote since 1975, when both voter and recipient sit in the same regional bloc - Nordic, ex-Yugoslav, ex-Soviet, or the Greek-Cypriot pair - the average points awarded is 5.26. 16When the recipient is outside that bloc, the average is 2.31.

17The Greek-Cypriot pair is the most extreme: their internal average vote is 10.98 points, almost five times what they give anyone else. 18The ex-Yugoslav bloc averages 7.40 inside, 1.99 outside. 19Nordics: 4.88 vs 2.19.

20Look at the lifetime sum of points flowing between specific pairs and the same map redraws itself. 21Cyprus to Greece: 394 points across 47 years. 22Denmark to Sweden: 366. 23Norway to Sweden: 360. 24The top thirty country-to-country flows are dominated by Nordic-internal trades, the Greek-Cypriot bilateral, and the Iberian and Anglo-Irish exchanges.

26The 2009 reform barely moved the needle

27When the EBU re-introduced 50/50 jury+televote scoring in 2009, the explicit goal was to dilute Eastern-bloc and other regional alliances - to bring back the song.

28The data say it shaved bloc bias only modestly. 29Pre-2009: in-bloc votes averaged 5.61, out-of-bloc 2.54, ratio 2.21. 30Post-2009: 5.03 vs 2.10, ratio 2.40.

31The absolute gap shrank by half a point. 32The proportional gap - how much more your bloc gives you than strangers do - actually grew. 33Bloc voting persisted in roughly the same shape; the reform reshuffled which entries win, not how regions vote for each other.

34Ukraine 2022: the public outvoted the jury 2 to 1

35On May 14, 2022, three months after Russia's full-scale invasion, Kalush Orchestra performed 'Stefania' under Italian lights in Turin. 36The juries placed them fourth, behind the United Kingdom, Sweden, and Spain. 37The televote placed them first by a chasm.

38Inside Ukraine's 631-point winning total, the dataset records a 192-to-432 split. 39Jurors gave the song 192 points - 39 voting countries handed out only 5 maximum scores. 40The public gave 432 points: 28 of 39 televoting countries awarded 12.

41Frontman Oleh Psiuk closed the performance by pleading for the world to free the Ukrainian fighters trapped under the Azovstal steel plant in Mariupol. 42The EBU declined to penalize the band, calling the comments humanitarian rather than political.

45When the jury and the public picked different shows

46Ukraine 2022 is dramatic but not unique. 47Since the EBU started publishing split jury and televote scores in 2016, several entries have finished at opposite ends of the two rankings.

48Poland's 'Color Of Your Life' was 25th of 26 by the jury in 2016 and 3rd by the public - a 22-place spread, the largest in the split-score era. 49Australia 2017 went the other way (4th jury, 25th public). 50Sweden 2018 (2nd jury, 23rd public) and Moldova 2022 (20th jury, 2nd public) are the next-largest gaps.

51These are not music-criticism gaps. They are sociology.

52Sweden gets 12 points more than anyone

53Sweden has been awarded 12 points 110 times since 1975 - more than any other country. 54Ukraine is second (83), the United Kingdom third (73), Italy fourth (72), Greece fifth (70).

55Sweden's tally is partly Nordic bloc gravity - Denmark, Norway, Iceland, and Finland all rank Sweden among their lifetime favorites - but it is also actual wins. 56With six Eurovision victories and ABBA's 'Waterloo' as the contest's most-celebrated song, Sweden's place in the top of the table is structural.

57Across the full record, Ireland leads with 7 wins (1970, 1980, 1987, 1992, 1993, 1994, 1996), Sweden is second with 6, and only 27 of the 52 ever-competing countries have won at all.

60Three wins that defined what Eurovision could be

62Three winners anchor the contest's cultural memory.

63ABBA's 'Waterloo' won Eurovision 1974 for Sweden with 24 points - the modest tally is an artifact of the era's voting cap. 64The song was later voted the best in the contest's history.

65Lordi's 'Hard Rock Hallelujah' won 2006 for Finland on 292 points - a record at the time, performed in monster prosthetics, the country's only Eurovision victory.

66Austria's Conchita Wurst won 2014 with 'Rise Like a Phoenix' on 290 points. 67The win, by a drag artist in a beard, became one of Eurovision's most-cited statements on LGBTQ+ visibility in Europe.

72The view from 1956

73Eurovision started in 1956 with seven countries. 74Through the late 1980s the field hovered between 18 and 22. 75Then the Soviet Union fell and Yugoslavia broke up, and the count climbed: 26 by 2003, 36 in the first semi-final year of 2004, 43 by 2008.

76The geographic center of gravity moved with the field. 77Through the 1990s, no Eastern European country had ever won. 78From 2000 to 2009, five did - Estonia 2001, Latvia 2002, Ukraine 2004, Serbia 2007, Russia 2008 - and Western Europe won zero. 79It is the only decade in 66 years in which the Western bloc was shut out completely.

80The 2010s redistributed: Western 4, Nordic 3, Eastern 2, Mediterranean 1. 81Ukraine 2022 then made it Eastern Europe's first 2020s win. 82The map keeps moving.

83Cyprus and Greece have given each other 12 points 50 times. 84Moldova and Romania, 22. 85Denmark and Sweden, 17. 86Norway and Sweden, 17. 87Portugal and Spain, 13. 88Eight of the top twelve mutual-12-point pairs lie within the same regional bloc.

89None of this means Eurovision is rigged. 90It means Eurovision was never only a song competition. 91It was always a song competition stacked on top of a televised diplomatic exchange between national broadcasters and their viewing publics, and the data reveal both layers at once.

92When Cyprus gives Greece 12 points, what is being measured is not 'best song'. 93It is something older and stranger: a 47-year unbroken civic ritual that says, every May, we vote for our neighbors. 94The song is the excuse. 95The points are the message.
