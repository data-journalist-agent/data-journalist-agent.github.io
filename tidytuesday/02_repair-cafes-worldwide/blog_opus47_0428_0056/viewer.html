2What 178,749 Repair Cafe visits tell us about the things we throw away

3A 12-year, 25-country, 447-cafe log of community repair: who shows up, what they bring, what gets fixed — and whether GenAI is changing the way we mend.

1. 4The world is drowning in barely-broken stuff

5Humans generated a record 662 million tonnes of e-waste in 20227, and the figure is on track to hit 82 Mt by 2030 — only 22.3% is currently formally collected and recycled, leaving roughly US$62 billion of recoverable material in landfill every year. 8Against that backdrop, a Dutch journalist named Martine Postma started a small, free, walk-in workshop in Amsterdam on 18 October 2009 where neighbours could bring broken kettles and torn jeans for volunteer fixers to mend. 9Sixteen years later, the Repair Cafe International Foundation counts more than 103,800 active branches across 40+ countries.

11This blog is built on RepairMonitor, the Foundation's own logging platform, which volunteers fill in after every visit. 12The 2026-04-07 TidyTuesday release of that dataset captures 13178,749 repair visits, 447 cafes, 25 countries, 1,951 distinct products and 8,600 named brands across 12 years. 14It is the closest thing the world has to a longitudinal record of community repair.

2. 15Fix rate: 63% — almost identical to the Foundation's own benchmark

16Of the 178,749 logged visits, 112,775 — exactly 1763.09% 18— ended in a successful repair, with 24.1% failed and 12.8% partially fixed. 19That is one percentage point above the Foundation's 2024 factsheet figure of 62%, and within a point of the Restart Project's Fixometer dataset (~60%). 20So the headline rate is stable across years, platforms and countries: 21roughly two out of three things that walk in the door walk out working again.

2263.09% 23of all 178,749 logged visits ended in a successful repair — 112,775 items saved from landfill.

26The dataset is not balanced geographically. 27The Netherlands alone contributes 50.8% of all rows; the top three countries (NL, GB, FR) together make up 2882.9%. 29Country fix rates vary in a believable band — US 70.9%, GB 65.5%, NL 62.3%, FR 55.6% — but the Dutch tilt means cross-country claims should be made cautiously.

3. 30What's easy, what's hard: a 70-percentage-point gap

31The variation that really matters is not between countries but between categories. 32Textiles get fixed 92.3% of the time33; tools without electronics 90.0%; jewelry 82.9%; bicycles 81.7%. 34At the other end, the largest single bucket — household electric appliances (70,983 visits) — fixes only 55.3%. 35Display & sound equipment (TVs, hi-fi, monitors) is the worst major category at 3645.8%.

39Drill down to individual products and the gap widens to 70 percentage points. 40With at least 500 visits each, the easiest items to fix are knives and scissors (4197.5%42), T-shirts (96.2%), trousers (96.0%), pruning shears (95.4%), and dresses (94.7%). 43The hardest are televisions (4429.7%45), milk frothers (30.4%), video recorders (31.6%), printers (36.8%), and electric kettles (39.9%). 46Textile vs electronic. 47Mechanical vs digital. 48Repairable-by-design vs not.

4. 50The movement quadrupled after the pandemic — but the fix rate stayed flat

51Annual logged visits rose from 28 in 2015 to 15,491 in 2019, then collapsed during COVID (6,686 in 2020, 5,813 in 2021), then surged: 15,096 in 2022, 29,406 in 2023, 39,981 in 2024, 45,165 in 2025. 52That is a 4.2x increase between pre-pandemic 2019 and 2025. 53The fix rate, meanwhile, has hovered in a tight 60-66% band every year since 2017 — a strikingly stable property of the format, even as the underlying volume changed by a factor of four.

56This rapid growth coincides with the policy backdrop maturing in real time. 57The EU adopted its Right to Repair Directive in April 2024, requiring spare parts for up to 10 years and repair info at fair prices. 58New York's Digital Fair Repair Act has been in force since July 2023; California's SB 244 since July 2024.

5. 59Why things don't get fixed: spare parts, parts, and parts

60Among the 26,537 failed-or-partial visits with a recorded reason, the headline barriers are 'no way to fix the product' (6120.9%62), 'spare parts not available at the session' (17.7%), 'unidentified failure' (13.8%), 'too worn out' (12.6%) and 'no way to open the product' (8.7%). 63Combine the three spare-parts buckets — at session, on the market, too expensive — and parts availability accounts for 34.1% of all failure mentions, by far the largest single barrier. 64This is precisely the lever the EU directive targets.

67The pattern is sharper still in specific categories: spare-parts mentions account for 6817.8% 69of failed electrical tools and 16.4% of failed household electrics, but under 1% of textile failures. 70Volunteers' subjective 1-10 'repairability' score tracks outcome almost monotonically (7113.7% fixed at score 1, 85.8% at score 1072), which is partly a retrospective rationalisation but also a clean signal that reachability of internals predicts outcome.

6. 73Median item is 8 years old; brand fix rates span 50+ points

74Where the production year is recorded (36% of rows), the median estimated year of production is 2014 — meaning the typical brought-in item is 758 years old at repair76, with a mean of 13.8 years. 77Counterintuitively, fix rate inches up with age: 0–4 year-olds 59.5%, 50+ year-olds 63.4%. 78Old things tend to be mechanical things; new things tend to be screen-based electronics.

79Brand-level numbers tell the same story from a manufacturer angle. 80Among brands with 300+ visits, the worst fix rates are 81Canon 34.1%, LG 35.3%, Bose 36.3%82, JVC 39.2%, Nespresso 41.9%, Samsung 42.5%; the best are bicycle and sewing brands (Batavus 86.5%, Gazelle 83.6%, Pfaff 73.8%, Singer 67.3%). 83Philips, the largest named brand at 10,649 visits, sits at 54.4%.

7. 86Is GenAI replacing YouTube as the repair info source? 87Not yet — but it's no longer zero

88TidyTuesday's prompt asked an open question: is GenAI overtaking YouTube as the go-to source of repair information for fixers? The free-text repair_info_source and repair_info_url 89fields contain only 7,191 non-null entries (4% of the dataset), but within them YouTube dominates with 90879 mentions91, followed by manufacturer manuals (385), Google search (279), iFixit (112) and forums (64). 92GenAI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Perplexity, Bing Chat) appear 9332 times in total.

96The growth curve is unmistakable: 970 mentions before 2023, then 3 in 2023, 10 in 2024, 17 in 2025, and 2 in early 2026. Sample entries — "chat gpt", "WWW.OPENai.COM", 98"Datasheet internet et chatgpt", "AI Perplexity" 99— read like a small ethnography of new tools entering an old practice.

101But the answer to the original question is clearly no, not yet102: in 2025 YouTube was mentioned 239 times to GenAI's 17, a 14-to-1 ratio. 103The replacement story will become true some day; the data say it is not true today.
